What the filter enforces

  • The filter runs on the server and builds a separate view for each family member. The family app never receives data the level hides.
  • Scam warnings, SOS alerts, 911 calls and Ruth's answers to check-ins reach family at every level.
  • Family cannot call into Doodad or turn on its camera or mic. Sarah can send a check-in request, and Ruth decides whether to answer.
  • Every view is written to the access log. Ruth can ask “Who saw my things?” and Doodad reads it back. Level changes go to the consent ledger.
  • Partner offers appear only in the family app, labeled Partner offer, with the referral fee disclosed. Ruth never hears an offer. Doodad takes no per-move-in facility bounties and no referral fees on covered health services.
  • Doodad never sells data.

The smartphone MVP ships first with the same look-and-talk flow and family app sync. The handheld uses the same pipeline.

Section 2

Senior speech data and the model moat

Every request to Doodad passes through speech recognition first. General-purpose models make more errors on older voices, so this gap affects every interaction Ruth has.

Word error rate by speaker age

Preliminary CPU run, base Whisper, CORAAL. Senior speakers had a word error rate 9.5 points higher.

The plan

Fine-tune a senior-tuned speech model with LoRA adapters on an open base model, starting from the proof of concept above.

Serve it as three tiers. Simple commands (calls, the TV remote, reminders, yes or no) run on the device and work offline. Our own senior-tuned models, running in our cloud, handle most other requests: speech, labels, mail. Only hard or high-stakes requests, like a suspected scam the model is unsure of, go to a frontier model. Each request our models keep costs a fraction of a frontier call, and we are not exposed to API price changes.

Then extend the same method to how seniors phrase requests (“the thing on the TV went away”), to reading labels on pill bottles and mail, and to the scam pattern library.

Public corpora contain little speech from older adults giving commands to a home device. Each Doodad user who opts in adds that kind of data, and a general-purpose model does not have it.

Where the data comes from, in order

  1. 01Proof of concept

    Public corpora

    • Common Voice, age-tagged
    • CORAAL
    • Santa Barbara Corpus
    • DementiaBank VAS corpus: older adults speaking to a voice assistant, the closest match to command language

    Research license terms apply to DementiaBank data.

  2. 02Pilot, plan

    Paid, consented recording

    Recorded through a remote panel such as Prolific. About $700 for a 50-speaker pilot and $20–25k for 1,000 speakers.

    Panel pools skew younger and healthier than Doodad's users, so recruiting screens for age.

  3. 03In product, plan

    Opt-in product data

    Doodad users who say yes to “help Doodad understand older voices.” Recordings are de-identified and the senior can revoke consent at any time. This consent is separate from family sharing.

    Voice can count as biometric data under some state laws, for example Illinois BIPA. Collection uses written consent and a retention schedule.

The flywheel

Guardrails

  • Opt-in only, and separate from the family sharing level.
  • Voice data is never sold.
  • Voice data is never used for ads.
  • Partner offers to family are not driven by voice recordings.
  • When Ruth deletes her data, training copies are removed at the next model refresh.
  • Ruth can ask Doodad “Are you saving my voice?” and get a true answer from her consent record.
